Interactive AI-Powered Robot

Real-time voice + expressive motion on an open-source, low-cost chassis (ESP32 + Qwen API).

Context
NYU Shanghai · IMA
Focus
HCI · Product design · Prototyping
Stack
ESP32, Qwen API, Servos, 3D-printed parts
Year
2025

Case

Most talking toys play pre-recorded lines. This robot listens, answers in unscripted speech, and gestures while it talks.

Task

Design the behavior model and motion language; implement the voice pipeline (capture → Qwen API → TTS) on ESP32; integrate servos and a 3D-printed body; iterate through user tests.

What it is

The Interactive AI Robot is a plush toy with real-time voice interaction and basic motion. An ESP32 microcontroller handles the audio and calls the Qwen API to generate speech, so its replies are unscripted and it moves as it speaks.

The project tests whether pairing language generation with physical motion makes a toy more engaging as a companion.

Market context

Open Securities projects that China's AI plush toy market will reach 32.3B RMB by 2027 in its neutral scenario.

Globally, the companion robot market was about 75B RMB in 2023 and is expected to reach 304.3B RMB by 2029, a CAGR of 25.6%.

As AI becomes more accessible, toys can do more for communication, learning and emotional development, especially for children and older adults who benefit from frequent engagement.

Comparable products

显眼包 (ByteDance): an AI-themed wearable with a facial display and limited audio. It is not sold commercially, has no motion or speech generation, and responds only with static emoji.

Talking Tom (plush): about €19.99 (¥158). It mimics the user's voice and plays pre-recorded phrases, with no AI processing or physical interaction.

Interactive AI Robot (this project): an estimated ¥200 to ¥300. It combines live voice recognition, AI-generated speech and basic motion.

Design and motion

The mechanical design builds on the open-source OTTO Robot platform, an affordable base for humanoid-style motion.

Servo motors driven by the ESP32 sync gestures (nod, wave, head turn) with speech events from the Qwen API pipeline (capture → NLU → TTS), so voice and motion happen together.

Credits

Thanks to the OTTO DIY Robot community for open-source inspiration.

Tech stack: ESP32, Qwen API, servo drivers, and 3D-printed casing.
