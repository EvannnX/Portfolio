Spiral Genesis

A kinetic sculpture that sets organic spiral forms in mechanical motion.

Context
NYU Shanghai · IMA
Focus
Kinetic art · Mechanical design · Sculpture
Stack
Arduino, servos, 3D printing, metal fabrication
Year
2025

Case

Build a kinetic sculpture that moves in spiral patterns, balancing organic form against mechanical precision.

Task

Design and fabricate the sculpture and its programmed motion system, then stage it as an exhibition piece.

Concept

Spiral Genesis starts from spiral forms found in nature, such as nautilus shells and galaxy arms, and rebuilds that growth pattern as programmed mechanical motion.

The form and the mechanism were designed together, so the piece sits between art and engineering. It asks how a human-made mechanism relates to natural design.

Structure

The structure had to move smoothly and stay stable. Each part supports the moving elements and is also part of how the piece looks.

The main engineering problems:

  • Integrating the servo motors precisely enough for fluid motion
  • Distributing weight so the movement stays balanced
  • Mounts durable enough for an exhibition
  • A modular build that is easy to maintain and update

Motion

An Arduino program drives the servos through choreographed sequences, with smooth transitions between spiral configurations.

Exhibition

In the exhibition, visitors could watch the mechanism up close.

It drew both art and technology audiences, and started conversations about the place of technology in contemporary art.

What the project taught

The project combined mechanical engineering, motion programming and sculpture, and took many rounds of prototyping and testing.

  • Mechanical design and fabrication
  • Motion programming and servo control
  • Exhibition design
  • Working across art and engineering
